# Campaign 2, Dedicated Build B — Kṛṣṇa: the control that bites nowhere

Run 2026-11-17 against `campaign2/CAMPAIGN2-PREREG.md` §B. This is
Campaign 2's deliberate expected-NO-BITE control, as Śaṅkara was
Campaign 1's (Case 15). It is run with the same work as every other
case: the channels are searched, the finds are inventoried, and the
negative is *earned*, not assumed. A campaign whose method cannot
return an honest zero on Kṛṣṇa could not be trusted when it returns
a 31-year correction on Kashmir.

**The questions.** (B1) Does any Chinese source — pilgrim record,
dynastic annal, Chinese-translated Buddhist literature — mention
Kṛṣṇa, the Mahābhārata war, or Kurukṣetra, and does any mention
carry a date? (B2) If not, what evidence class actually bears on
the war's traditional date (3139 BCE) and its scholarly brackets?

---

## B1 — The inventory: Kṛṣṇa is in the canon, and it proves nothing

The honest answer begins with a concession: **Kṛṣṇa is present in
Buddhist literature, including literature that reached China.**
What matters is the form of the presence.

**(i) The Jātaka stratum — Kṛṣṇa as past-life furniture.** The
*Ghaṭa Jātaka* (Pali Jātaka no. 454) retells the whole Mathurā
cycle as a birth-story: Kaṃsa, Devagabbhā (= Devakī), Vāsudeva,
Baladeva, Govaddhana (= Govardhana), and Kaṇha/Kesava (= Kṛṣṇa
Keśava) all appear — recast, as the Dasaratha Jātaka recasts Rāma,
into a tale whose point is a Buddhist moral about grief. The
*Vidhurapaṇḍita Jātaka* knows Madhurā (Mathurā). Birth-story
collections of this stratum circulated into Chinese translation
(the jātaka/avadāna compilations of the 3rd–5th centuries). Grade
this material exactly as Campaign 1 graded the Aśoka legends: it
is *narrative cargo*. It carries no reign, no era, no regnal year,
no synchronism — a jātaka is set "in a former age" by design, and
its Chinese translation date (where a given collection has one)
dates the *story's circulation*, exactly as the *Ayuwang zhuan*'s
306 CE dates the Aśoka legend stratum in Build A. Nobody's
chronology has ever been moved by it, in either direction.

**(ii) The cosmological stratum — Kṛṣṇa as a deva-name.** In
Mahāyāna cosmology and dhāraṇī literature, Kṛṣṇa (Chinese *Heitian*,
"Black Deva") survives as a class/name among devas and demon-kings
in the Buddha's assemblies — a piece of the shared Indian pantheon
that Buddhist texts demoted to audience. A name in an assembly list
is not a person in a record: no Chinese text gives this figure a
kingdom, a century, or an act that an annal could date.

**(iii) The annals — silence.** The *Shiji*, *Hanshu* and *Hou
Hanshu* accounts of Shendu/Tianzhu (India) record geography,
products, customs, and — by the *Hou Hanshu* — Buddhism itself
("the people cultivate the Way of the Buddha"). No king of India
is named in any of them before the embassy age, and Kṛṣṇa, the
Kurukshetra war, and the Pāṇḍavas appear in none.

**(iv) The pilgrims — the sharpest silence in this campaign.**
Faxian, Xuanzang and Yijing walked the epic's own geography and
filed reports on all of it. Xuanzang describes Mathurā (Mo-tu-lo)
at length — its monasteries, its non-Buddhist temples, its relic
cult — and never names Kṛṣṇa, in the city Indian tradition calls
Kṛṣṇa's own. He passes through Sthāṇeśvara (Thanesar), the city
on the edge of Kurukṣetra, and reports its wealth, its people and
its sacred tanks without one word of a world war fought on the
plain beside it or of the god-king who fought there. Yijing,
cataloguing Indian learning, names grammarians and logicians by
the dozen; no epic, no Kṛṣṇa. The Mahābhārata as a text was never
translated into Chinese in the premodern period and appears in no
Tang catalogue — contrast the Rāmāyaṇa *motifs* that leaked in
through jātakas, and the Buddhist texts that fill the catalogues
by the thousand. The channel that carried the *Zhong lun* in 409
and reported Bhartṛhari's death to the year carried no syllable of
dated Kṛṣṇa material, because there was no dated Kṛṣṇa material
in the channel's India to carry: what circulated was story and
cult, not record.

**Why the silence cannot be converted into a bound.** The campaign
refused this move for Śaṅkara and refuses it here, harder. An
argument "not attested in China by 700 CE, therefore late" would
require the channel to register Brahmanical epic tradition; the
spine's structural finding is that it registered Buddhist texts,
Buddhist pilgrims' observations, and court diplomacy — and the
epic reached it only in the one form Buddhism itself consumed,
the birth-story. Kṛṣṇa's absence from the *dated* record is
predicted on every hypothesis about his date from 3100 BCE to
900 BCE. A datum predicted by all hypotheses discriminates among
none.

## B2 — What actually bears on the war's date

Since the Chinese record reaches nothing here, the file states
plainly where the question really lives:

- **The traditional date, 3139/3102 BCE**, is not a transmitted
  memory but a computed epoch: the Kali Yuga era (epoch 3101 BCE,
  systematised by Āryabhaṭa's time, 499 CE) with the war placed at
  the Dvāpara–Kali junction, cross-summed against Purāṇic king
  lists. It is Indian astronomical-traditional arithmetic — the
  same evidence class as Fei Changfang's Buddha dates in Build A,
  and to be graded the same way wherever it appears.
- **The scholarly brackets** (proposals run from c. 1800 BCE down
  to a 9th–10th-century BCE core, c. 950 BCE being a frequently
  defended figure) rest on Indian evidence classes: the
  archaeology of the Kurukshetra–western Ganga region (Painted
  Grey Ware and successor horizons), the epic's own textual
  stratigraphy (a text accreted over centuries, c. 400 BCE–400 CE
  in its received form), iron-age material culture inside the
  poem, and palaeo-environmental arguments (the Sarasvatī/Ghaggar
  channel's desiccation). Greek testimony (Megasthenes' Herakles
  identifications) is sometimes conscripted; it is 3rd-century BCE
  ethnography about cult, not a war report.
- **The Chinese record's earliest notice of India** is Zhang
  Qian's report (returned 126 BCE; Campaign 2 Case D1) — roughly
  **three thousand years after the traditional war date**, and
  centuries after even the latest scholarly bracket for a
  historical kernel. The gap is not a failure of searching. It is
  structural: China's India archive opens with Han diplomacy, and
  nothing in it was ever going to reach the Bronze/Iron Age
  transition on the Yamuna.

**Verdict B: NO BITE** — on both sub-questions, as pre-registered,
and reported as a result, not a disappointment. The control's
yield is the map of the method's edge: the spine dates what China
touched — embassies, translations, pilgrims' presents — and
Kṛṣṇa's India is separated from China's first touch by millennia
and by a channel that carried him only as a story. The campaign
that can say this, with the inventory shown, is the campaign whose
CORRECTED verdicts mean something. **Firmness: Firm** (the
inventory and the catalogues are checkable; the negative is a
searched negative). **Strongest objection to the NO BITE itself:**
the *Ghaṭa Jātaka* material shows the Kṛṣṇa cycle was already
pan-Indian narrative property by the early centuries CE — could
its *circulation* bound the epic tradition's formation? It bounds
the *story*, as Build A's 306 CE bounds the Aśoka legend; the war
and the man are not the story, and the campaign does not let a
bound on a tale pose as a bound on an event.
