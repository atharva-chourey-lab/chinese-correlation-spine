## Addendum (2026-11-17, Atharva) — Scope E: Mahāvīra and the śramaṇa world

Added at the commissioner's word AFTER §§A–D were graded, and
registered here BEFORE any E case is graded. Dedicated build
(deliverable: `MAHAVIRA.md`) plus sweep cases:

- E1. **Mahāvīra himself.** Traditional dates (Śvetāmbara 599–527
  BCE; Digambara variants; the "470 years before the Vikrama era"

  the Buddha: the two traditions cross-refer (Nigaṇṭha Nātaputta in

  never travelled to China, so the annals are the expected NO BITE

  Kaccāyana, Sañjaya Belaṭṭhiputta, Nigaṇṭha Nātaputta): what
  exactly is attested, and does any translation date, run through
  the spine's lag discipline, bound the stratum?
- E3. **The pilgrims' snapshot.** Xuanzang and Yijing describe
  Nirgranthas/Jains (and possibly Ājīvikas) in 7th-century India —
  a dated snapshot of the śramaṇa world's survival. Grade what it
  pins.
- E4. **Pārśva** (traditionally 250 years before Mahāvīra): any
  bite at all, or pure chain arithmetic? Grade it as what it is.
- E5. **Makkhali Gosāla and the Ājīvikas** as their own case
  (Aśokan edicts are Indian-side; is there a Chinese side?).
- E6. **Ālāra Kālāma and Uddaka Rāmaputta** — the Buddha's
  teachers. Expected NO BITE; registered and graded, not skipped.

## Method (unchanged from Campaign 1)

The Chinese record governs the event it directly records; a
colophon gives a TAQ for the Indian original minus an unknown lag
(empirical lag: median ≈ 160–186 yrs, provisional, n=5 solid);
a pilgrim dates what he saw; reported intervals inside pilgrim,
biographical and catalogue texts are tradition wearing the record's
clothes and are graded as tradition wherever they appear —
including when they arrive in Chinese, and including when the
Chinese tradition itself is the fabricator (Case A2).
New anchors enter only through `spine.py add`; the test suite runs
after every merge.
