# Campaign 2, Dedicated Build A — The Buddha: the full chronology war

Run 2026-11-17 against `campaign2/CAMPAIGN2-PREREG.md` §A. Campaign 1
Case 1 (the dotted record) is **not** re-graded: its verdict — NO BITE,
because the record's custodian chain is the Theravāda Vinaya lineage
itself, so its 486 BCE is the Ceylon tradition testifying about
itself — stands and is cited below as settled.

**The question.** When did the Buddha die? Three live answers:
the corrected long chronology, **486/483 BCE** (Theravāda interval:
Aśoka consecrated 218 years after the parinirvāṇa; Aśoka's
consecration Greek-anchored at c. 268 BCE; 268 + 218 = 486);
the short chronology, **c. 368 BCE** (the 100-year interval of the
non-Theravāda Vinayas; 268 + 100 = 368); and the Bechert symposium
consensus, **c. 400 ± 30 BCE**, which trusts neither interval whole.
The Aśoka peg is firm and is *not* Chinese — it is the Greek
synchronism of Rock Edict 13. Everything else is the interval.
This build asks what the Chinese record does to the interval war.

---

## A1 — The two intervals inside the Chinese transmission itself

**What China actually carries.** Both rival intervals reached us
*through* Chinese translation, each as the cargo of a lineage:

| Interval | Carrier tradition | Chinese-translated witness | Translation date (TAQ for the stratum) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 218 years | Theravāda | *Samantapāsādikā* (Buddhaghosa's Vinaya commentary, carrying the Ceylon chronicle arithmetic) | trans. Saṅghabhadra, **488 CE** (the same Saṅghabhadra who placed the 975th dot at Canton in 489 — Campaign 1 Case 1) |
| 100 years | Sarvāstivāda / northern Vinayas | The northern Vinaya recensions and the Aśoka-legend cycle: Aśoka stands ~a century after the nirvāṇa, contemporary with Upagupta, a handful of generations down the patriarch line | *Ayuwang zhuan* (T.2042, the Aśokāvadāna), trans. An Faqin, **fl. 281–306 CE**; northern Vinayas translated in the first decades of the 5th c. |

**The derivation.** The translation dates are the only hard numbers
in this sub-case, and what they date is the *disagreement*, not the
event. By c. 306 CE the short-interval legend cycle was already
fully formed in a translatable Sanskrit text; by 488 CE the
long-interval Ceylon arithmetic was fully formed in another. The
two intervals were therefore **already lineage traditions, centuries
old and mutually exclusive, before either entered China** — China
is the archive that preserved both, not a witness for either. A
transmitted interval is exactly what Campaign 1's balance sheet
warned about: an Indian institution's own number travelling in
Chinese dress inherits that institution's construction, and two
lineages' numbers cannot arbitrate each other.

**One genuine bound falls out.** The *Ayuwang zhuan* translation
(c. 306 CE, re-translated 512 CE by Saṅghabhara as the *Ayuwang
jing*, T.2043) is a firm TAQ for the Aśoka-legend stratum — the
84,000-stūpa cycle, the Upagupta frame, the gift-of-dirt prophecy —
as a *formed literary tradition*. Any argument that treats the
Aśoka legends as late-medieval accretion is dead by 306; any
argument that treats them as record of the 3rd century BCE must
still cross five centuries of unrecorded transmission on its own.

**Verdict A1: BOUNDED.** The Chinese record bounds the *strata*
(both intervals fully formed by c. 306–488 CE) and thereby proves
the interval war is a war of traditions, not of records. It does
not move the parinirvāṇa. **Firmness:** the translation dates, Firm
as catalogue/colophon record (An Faqin's floruit 281–306 is the
standard attribution); the inference from them, direct.
**Strongest objection:** a defender of the long chronology can note
that the 218 interval's carrier is a *Vinaya commentary* — a
discipline text with a custodial chain — while the 100 interval's
carriers are avadāna legend; on a genre-weighting, the two strata
are not symmetric. The campaign's answer stands from Case 1: the
commentary's chain and the chronicle's sums are the same
institution's arithmetic, and genre does not manufacture
independence.

## A2 — China's own Buddha dates: the correction runs in reverse

**The Indian-side estimate** is not the target here. The target is
a set of dates that circulate to this day as "the Chinese
traditional dating of the Buddha," as if China were a witness.
It was not a witness. It was a manufacturer, and its products
disagree with each other by centuries:

- **The Zhao/Mu scheme.** The Buddha born in the 24th–26th year of
  King Zhāo of Zhōu — **c. 1029–1027 BCE** — and died in King Mù's
  52nd year, **c. 949 BCE**. The source cited for it in the debate
  literature is the *Zhōu Shū Yìjì* ("Records of the Zhou
  Documents"), a text no one has ever produced, quoted in a court
  debate preserved through the biographical tradition (the monk
  Tanwu/Dharma-figure citing it against a Daoist opponent under the
  Northern Wei). Note the scheme's internal fit: 1027 − 949 = 78,
  i.e. the Indian tradition's ~80-year lifespan, bolted onto the
  Zhōu king list at a point chosen for the portents (a star's
  appearance, an earthquake) the legend required.
- **Fei Changfang's constructions.** The *Lidai Sanbao Ji* (597 CE)
  — the same catalogue that preserves the dotted record — records
  **several** backward-counting scales from 597, from which death
  dates as early as **1005 BCE** can be derived; and Fei's own
  imperial-chronology section opens with the Buddha's birth in the
  *jiawu* year of King Zhuāng of Zhōu, **687 BCE**. One catalogue,
  two birth years **340 years apart** (1027 vs 687).
- **The later surveyors knew.** Zanning's *Da Song Seng Shi Lüe*
  (999 CE) reviews the competing schemes, remarks on the
  disagreement, and picks the King Mù chronology anyway — a
  preference, recorded as a preference.

**Why the dates were built.** Two pressures, both Chinese, neither
evidentiary: the *mofa* (Dharma-decline) countdown, which needed a
parinirvāṇa date to compute how much time remained; and the
Laozi-*huahu* polemic, in which the Buddha's date relative to
Laozi's was a debating weapon between Buddhist and Daoist court
factions. A date manufactured to win a debate and to start a
countdown is not a measurement, and its agreement with nothing —
not even with the catalogue printed beside it — is the proof.

**The arithmetic of exclusion.** Every Chinese traditional scheme
places the parinirvāṇa between c. 1050 and c. 650 BCE. Every
Indian-side candidate, including the uncorrected Ceylon figure
(544/543), is later than 550 BCE. The Chinese schemes are therefore
not a fourth candidate in the war of §A1; they are off its map
entirely, by 100–500 years, and mutually inconsistent by up to
~350 years (death dates derivable from Fei's own pages alone span
1005 BCE down toward the 7th century).

**Verdict A2: CORRECTED — in the reverse direction.** Campaign 1
corrected Indian dates with Chinese records. This case corrects a
*Chinese* tradition with the campaign's own method: the "Chinese
dating of the Buddha" (1027 BCE and its siblings) is graded a
Chinese-side construction, excluded from evidence, and any modern
work that cites it as an independent witness repeats a Sui-dynasty
cataloguer's arithmetic as if it were a record. **Firmness: Firm**
(the texts, their dates, and their mutual inconsistency are all
directly checkable). **Strongest objection:** Fei Changfang
*recorded* scales he did not invent — the Zhao/Mu scheme is older
than his catalogue. Granted, and it changes nothing: a construction
recorded in 597 was constructed before 597; the verdict grades the
scheme, not the scribe.

## A3 — The sharpest defensible bound

Assemble what survives grading:

1. Aśoka's consecration: **c. 268 ± 5 BCE** — Greek synchronism,
   firm, non-Chinese. (The spine's sheet anchor.)
2. The dotted record: excluded (Campaign 1 Case 1 — dependent).
3. The 218 interval: one lineage's sum, transmitted 488 CE (A1).
4. The 100/110 interval: the rival lineages' sum, transmitted from
   c. 306 CE (A1).
5. China's own dates: excluded (A2 — constructions).

**The sharpest defensible statement the Chinese record permits:**
the parinirvāṇa lies in **[c. 500, c. 350] BCE** on the total
evidence — and the Chinese record narrows *nothing inside that
interval*. It cannot choose 486 over 400 over 368, because every
Chinese-carried number that touches the question is either a
lineage's own interval (not independent), a lineage's own dot
count (not independent), or a Chinese construction (not evidence).
What will decide the question, if anything does, is Indian-side:
the archaeology of the second urbanisation, the stratigraphy of
the early texts, and the internal criticism of the two intervals —
the ground on which the Bechert consensus (c. 400 ± 30) already
stands. China's contribution to the Buddha's date is real but
negative-positive, and it is the campaign's cleanest statement of
its own law: **China dates the carriers, not the carried.** The
legend strata are bounded (by 306, by 488); the man is not.

**Verdict A3: NO BITE** on the choice among 486 / 400 / 368.
**Firmness: Firm** — a NO BITE reached by exhausting the channels,
not by failing to look.
