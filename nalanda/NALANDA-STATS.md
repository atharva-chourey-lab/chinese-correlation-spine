# NALANDA, STATISTICALLY

*Every number the sources give for Nalanda, extracted, graded, and tested against each other and against the ground. A correlation-web build: Nalanda's best witnesses are spine witnesses (Xuanzang, Yijing), and its numbers had never been assembled as one dataset. Dataset: `numbers.csv` (49 rows). New spine anchors: `new-anchors/` (9 records, schema-validated).*

**Headline results, up front**

- The three 7th-century witnesses **agree exactly on the countable built environment** — eight halls (Xuanzang, Huili, Yijing independently), and Yijing's "more than 300 apartments" matches the excavated ground-floor cell estimate (~250–320). They **diverge by a factor of ~3 only on the crowd**.
- The famous **10,000 is not Xuanzang's figure**. His own Records say "several thousand." The 10,000 is his disciple Huili's, explicitly counts *residents and visitors together*, and exceeds the excavated residential plant's absolute ceiling by a factor of 5 or more.
- The **capacity ceiling** of the excavated core is ~450–650 residents at design occupancy (one monk per cell — the cells have one bed platform each), and under 2,000 even at dormitory crowding of 3 per cell.
- **Yijing is the anchor witness**: >3,000 residents (673–685), internally consistent with his own endowment figure (200+ villages → ~15 monks per village) and with the Devapāla plate's epigraphic endowment rate (~6–12 per village). Even his figure needs the unexcavated half of the walled complex — but it is carriable; Huili's is not.
- **Defensible peak resident population: c. 2,500–4,500, centred on Yijing's 3,000–3,500** (7th century). The series ends not in 1193 but in **1235**, with Dharmasvāmin's counted ~70 students under a 90-year-old abbot in a damaged but functioning institution.

Grades used throughout: **FIRM** (dated, inspectable, or independently corroborated) / **PROBABLE** (credible witness, some rounding or inference) / **RHETORIC** (round, panegyric, late, or unsourced) / **IMPOSSIBLE-BY-CAPACITY** (exceeds the physical plant under any stated occupancy assumption).

## 1. Source census — every dated witness that gives Nalanda a number

In date order. "Number-bearing" is the criterion: a witness who was there but counted nothing is listed too, because the gap is data.

| # | Witness | Date | What it numbers | Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Faxian, *Foguo Ji* | c. 410 | Nothing — Nalanda is a village with Śāriputra's stupa | Eyewitness; the series' zero point, just before foundation |
| 2 | Xuanzang, *Da Tang Xiyu Ji* | compiled 646; resident 637–642 | Priests "several thousand"; 8 halls; gate failure 7–8 of 10; 80-ft copper Buddha of Pūrṇavarman | Eyewitness, the master himself |
| 3 | Huili (compl. Yancong), *Life of Xuanzang* | c. 665–688 | 10,000 residents+visitors; 100 pulpits; mastery tiers 1,000/500/~10; ~100 villages; 200 householders per village; the guest-ration schedule | Disciple's panegyric biography — the inflation enters here |

Two structural facts about this census. First, **every population number for Nalanda's peak comes from exactly three men in one 50-year window** (637–691), two of whom were master and disciple. There is no Indian-side count at any date: no inscription ever numbers the sangha. Second, the epigraphic record — the hardest evidence class — numbers only *things given* (villages, endowments, buildings), never people. Where stone and pilgrims can be compared (endowment rates, §5), they can be made to check each other; on population, the pilgrims stand alone, which is why the capacity test (§3) matters.

The trajectory the dataset supports: foundation in the Gupta window; a walled multi-vihara complex by c. 600 housing hundreds; a 7th-century peak in the **low thousands** (the only century with counts); an uncounted but demonstrably functioning Pāla-era institution (foreign kings still building there in the 850s); decline without a dated cliff — the Persian chronicle usually cited for the sack does not name the site — and a long tail ending in a *counted* remnant of ~70 in 1235, forty years after the supposed annihilation. The textbook arc (10,000 at peak → total destruction c. 1193) is wrong at both ends: the peak is inflated ~3×, and the end is a fade documented by an eyewitness the textbooks rarely cite.

## 3. The capacity test — the excavated plant as a measuring instrument

**The instrument.** The ASI record (1915–37, 1974–82; UNESCO property) gives 11 viharas and 14 temples in a 23-ha protected area, the excavated strip ~12 ha. The site plaques transcribe per-monastery cell counts for six viharas:

| Vihara | Cells (plaque count) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Monastery 1 (upper level) | 34 | Largest; cells carry a single bed platform + book niche; lower level and 1A/1B annexes uncounted |
| Monastery 4 | 20 | Later phase; earlier phase at least double-storeyed |
| Monastery 5 | 22 | 3 + 3 + two rows of 8; the rear row may be grain stores → residential 14–22 |
| Monastery 6 | 64 | 32 cells × 2 preserved levels |
| Monastery 9 | 34 | |
| Monastery 11 | 31 | |
| **Counted total (6 of 11)** | **205** | |

Monasteries 7, 8, 10 are standard-plan quadrangles of the same ~30 × 38 m class as 9 and 11 but carry no plaque count; Monastery 1's lower level (the 9th-century Bālaputradeva monastery) adds a further partial cell ring.

**Derived cell totals** (assumptions stated):
- Ground floor, all 11 viharas: counted units average ~29 cells; assigning the uncounted units the observed 20–34 range and a partial lower level to Monastery 1 → **~250–320 ground-floor cells**. This converges, independently, on Yijing's eyewitness "more than 300 apartments" — the single best cross-validation in the dataset, and strong evidence that Yijing counted rooms the way an administrator counts them.
- Upper floors: staircases attest second storeys in Monasteries 1, 4, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11. If every vihara carried a full upper cell ring → **~450–650 cells total**. (Some upper floors were surely partial; the range already discounts that.)

**Occupancy.** The cells are ~3 m × 3 m with *one* bed platform and a book niche — designed occupancy is 1. Applying the brief's 1–3 range:

| Assumption | Residential capacity of the excavated core |
|---|---|
| 1 per cell (design) | **450–650** |
| 2 per cell | 900–1,300 |
| 3 per cell (dormitory crowding, against design) | 1,350–1,950 — absolute ceiling, still **< 2,000** |

**The verdict on the claims.**
- **Huili's 10,000 as a resident population: IMPOSSIBLE-BY-CAPACITY.** It needs 5–15× the excavated plant at any occupancy. Even read as he wrote it — residents *and visitors together* — it stands ~2–3× above the most generous all-in reconstruction (below).
- **Yijing's 3,000–3,500: exceeds the excavated core, but is carriable.** The excavated strip is not the whole site: Xuanzang's brick wall enclosed the entire convent, the mound field runs under and around modern Bargaon, and only ~12 of 23 protected hectares are excavated. The rescues, with the fraction of the claim each can carry:
  - *Unexcavated viharas*: if the full complex held ~2× the excavated cell stock (~900–1,300 cells), at 2/cell that carries 1,800–2,600 — roughly **two-thirds to four-fifths** of Yijing's figure on its own.
  - *Shared occupancy in the excavated core at peak*: moving from 1 to 2 per cell across the whole complex carries most of the remainder.
  - *Day scholars and lay servants*: real categories (the endowment fed more mouths than monks), but they inflate the *fed* population, not the *resident sangha* Yijing was counting; credit them with hundreds, not thousands.
  - *A town outside the wall*: Bargaon existed, but no witness puts students in it; carry ≈ 0 without evidence.
  - Net: Yijing's figure survives as the population of the **whole walled complex at ~2 per cell** — plausible crowding for a institution at its peak. Huili's figure cannot be rescued by the same means: it would need the unexcavated remainder to be 4× the excavated core *and* triple occupancy everywhere *and* his own endowment to feed them (§5). Each rescue is individually conceivable; stacked, they are a confession.
- **Phase caveat, stated plainly:** the excavated viharas are mostly late (Pāla-period) rebuilds on earlier footprints. But the rebuilds stand on the *same plan* — Monastery 7 shows three successive monasteries on one footprint — inside the same walled enclosure. The test measures the site's carrying envelope across its life, not one year's roster. That is the right instrument for claims made about the site's peak centuries.

## 4. The inflation coefficient — Xuanzang and Yijing as paired instruments

Two pilgrims, one generation apart, both long-term residents, describing the same institution. Wherever both report the same kind of quantity:

| Quantity | Xuanzang side | Yijing | Ratio | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Residents | *Records*: "several thousand" | >3,000 (Memoir 3,500) | ≈ 1.0 | **The master and the later pilgrim agree** |
| Residents | Huili: 10,000 (incl. visitors) | >3,000 | **3.3** (2.9 vs 3,500) | The disciple's biography is the outlier |
| Halls | 8 (Records and Life agree) | 8 | 1.0 | Exact agreement |
| Apartments | — (no count) | 300+ | — | Matches excavation (~250–320 ground-floor cells) |
| Endowed villages | Huili: ~100, *Harṣa's grant alone* | 200+, *cumulative from successive kings* | not a ratio | Different referents; a naive comparison would falsely convict Yijing of doubling — the referents differ, and the texts say so |
| Mastery tiers / pulpits | Huili: 1,000 / 500 / ~10; 100 pulpits | — (no counterpart) | — | Unpaired; graded on their own (round) merits |

**The inflation coefficient is ≈ 3, and it is located precisely**: not in "Xuanzang" as modern literature cites him, but in the step from Xuanzang's own *Records* to Huili's *Life*. The master wrote "several thousand"; the disciple's biography, written to glorify the master and his school, wrote a myriad. Yijing — who had read Xuanzang and had every motive of piety to match him — instead filed the administrator's number, and his number is the one the ground corroborates. Where the two instruments can be checked against a third (halls: 8 = 8; apartments: 300 ≈ excavated ~300), **they agree on everything countable and diverge only on the crowd** — the classic signature of crowd rhetoric rather than measurement error.

**Round-number census** (rhetoric detector). Of the Nalanda quantities in each witness, the share that are round hundreds/thousands:

- *Xuanzang, Records*: several thousand (round), 7–8 of 10 (a ratio — the least rhetorical figure in his passage), 8 halls (counted), 80 ft (round).
- *Huili, Life*: 10,000; 1,000; 500; 100 pulpits; 100 villages; 200 householders — **6 of 6 headline figures round**, the exception being the guest-ration schedule (120/20/20/1 oz/1 peck), which is precise because it is a list, not a magnitude.
- *Yijing*: 3,000+ (round, but with a manuscript variant and a second statement at 3,500 — administrative rounding, not liturgical), 300+ apartments (round hundred, corroborated by excavation), 8 halls (counted), 200+ villages (round), 400 texts (round), 500,000 ślokas (round — his one liturgical number, graded RHETORIC while his 400 texts stand).
- *Dharmasvāmin*: ~70 students (counted), 90+ years (round decade), 2 viharas (counted), 300 soldiers (round), 14 + **84** monasteries (84 is a canonical sacred number — the detector fires).

Pattern: **precision is inversely proportional to size.** Small numbers in these texts are counted (70 students, 34 cells, 8 halls); large numbers are liturgical (10,000; 500,000; 84). Any Nalanda figure ≥ 1,000 from a literary source should be read as an order-of-magnitude claim — "thousands" — and graded accordingly.

## 5. Endowment and provisions — the carrying-capacity cross-check

Village-grant claims in date order:

| Date | Claim | Source | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| (reported c. 665–688) | Harṣa remits ~**100 villages**; 200 householders in each supply rice, butter and milk daily | Huili | PROBABLE as a grant; the levy mechanism RHETORIC |
| 691 (for 673–685) | **200+ villages**, accumulated from kings of successive generations | Yijing | PROBABLE |
| c. 725–752 | A permanent endowment (*akṣaya-nīvī*) funds *daily* distribution of rice, curds, ghee at Bālāditya's temple | Yaśovarman inscription | FIRM (the endowment; no rate stated) |
| c. 849–861 | **5 villages** for the upkeep of *one* vihara (Bālaputradeva's), incl. text-copying | Devapāla plate | FIRM |

**The per-village rate is where the witnesses convict or acquit each other.** A monastery's real carrying capacity is (villages × monks supportable per village). The sources imply three different rates:

- **Devapāla plate (epigraphic, FIRM):** 5 villages endow one vihara of ~30–60 residents → **~6–12 monks per village**.
- **Yijing (eyewitness):** 3,000+ monks on 200+ villages → **~15 monks per village**. Same order as the plate. ✓
- **Huili:** 10,000 on Harṣa's ~100 villages → **100 monks per village** — an order of magnitude above the epigraphic rate, from his own text's own numbers. ✗

Run it forward as a cross-check on the population series, at the epigraphically grounded rate of ~10–15 monks per village: Huili's own endowment (100 villages) supports **1,000–1,500** monks; Yijing's cumulative endowment (200+ villages) supports **2,000–3,000+** — landing exactly on Yijing's stated population and exactly under Huili's. Even at a generous 25 monks per village (a village of ~200 households surrendering a large share of its surplus), 100 villages feed 2,500, not 10,000. **Huili's population is refuted by Huili's endowment.** Yijing's two numbers, given in different chapters for different purposes, cohere — the mark of an administrator reporting one real institution.

Huili's levy mechanism deserves its own line: 200 householders × 100 villages = 20,000 households each delivering rice, butter and milk *daily*. Arithmetically it is scaled to his claim (20,000 daily contributions ≥ 10,000 mouths), but demographically it conscripts essentially every household in a hundred villages into daily service — a schematic (200 × 100), not a roster. Yijing's version of the same economy is the administrative form: landed revenue from 200+ villages, no per-household levy described.

**Provisions colour, graded.** Yijing's *Record* is the provisions source of real texture: the water-clock regulating the day, morning gruel and the midday meal, the rules for distributing food and requisites — procedures, not magnitudes, and graded PROBABLE throughout as eyewitness administrative description. Huili's one precise provisions datum is Xuanzang's honoured-guest allowance — 120 jāmbīra fruits, 20 areca nuts, 20 nutmegs, an ounce of camphor, a peck of Mahāśāli rice daily — PROBABLE as a ration schedule (it is a list, and lists in Huili are precise where his magnitudes are round), and useless for population, being one favoured guest's portion.

## 6. Library and curriculum numbers

| Claim | Earliest carrier | Grade | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dharmagañja library of 3 buildings (Ratnasāgara, Ratnodadhi, Ratnarañjaka) | Tibetan tradition, extant via Tāranātha (1608) | RHETORIC | No 7th-century witness names a library building. Xuanzang and Yijing describe texts, copying and study in detail — and never a library structure. The names may preserve genuine tradition; they are unverifiable and arrive 900 years after the peak they describe |
| Ratnodadhi of **9 storeys** | Same Tibetan tradition | RHETORIC | No excavated structure at Nalanda is identifiable as a nine-storey library; nothing in the ASI record approaches it |
| **9 million manuscripts** | None — modern literature only | RHETORIC (unsourced) | No pre-modern source carries this figure; it is sometimes falsely fathered on Tāranātha, who gives no count |
| Library burned for 3–6 months | Late tradition, hardened by modern repetition | RHETORIC | The *Tabaqat* does not name Nalanda and gives no burning duration; the months-long fire has no dated carrier |
| 657 texts carried to China | Xuanzang's own tradition (Life) | PROBABLE | A cargo, not a catalogue — but the firmest library-adjacent number in the file |
| 400 Sanskrit texts, 500,000 ślokas collected | Yijing (self-report, 691) | PROBABLE / RHETORIC | Split the pair: 400 texts is one pilgrim's plausible working haul; 500,000 ślokas is a round magnitude |
| Text-copying as a funded activity | Devapāla plate (c. 849–861) | FIRM | The plate endows "writing of the dharma-ratnas" alongside food and robes — the library's *function* is epigraphically real even though its architecture is not |
| **100 pulpits** lecturing daily | Huili | RHETORIC | Round; unpaired; at Yijing's 3,000 residents it would mean one simultaneous lecture per 30 monks — the number is a fullness trope |
| Curriculum breadth (Mahayana, 18 schools, Vedas, grammar, logic, medicine, Sāṃkhya…) | Xuanzang + Huili + Yijing converge | PROBABLE | A list, not a number — and the one curriculum claim all witnesses support |
| Study timelines (child begins the Siddham at 6, grammar stages by age…) | Yijing, *Record* | PROBABLE | Administrative detail from the pilgrim who also counted the rooms; the curriculum's *durations* are better witnessed than its enrolments |
| Gate failure 7–8 of 10 | Xuanzang, *Records* | PROBABLE | A ratio from the master himself; the origin of the modern "harder than Harvard" trope — it measures debate admission, not a university entrance exam |

The library section of the modern Nalanda legend is the dataset's weakest stratum: its architecture is 17th-century Tibetan tradition, its inventory is 20th-century invention, and its destruction scene is a composite. What survives grading is smaller and better: a working collection large enough that two pilgrims carried away 657 and 400 texts respectively, and a copying operation still being endowed by royal charter in the 850s.

### The defensible peak-population interval

Assembling only what survived grading:

- **Eyewitness bracket:** Xuanzang's "several thousand" (637–642) and Yijing's 3,000–3,500 (673–685) — two independent residents agreeing on the order of magnitude.
- **Capacity envelope:** the excavated core holds 450–650 at design occupancy; the whole walled complex, at the ~2-per-cell crowding Yijing's figure requires, holds roughly 2,000–4,000. A resident population materially above ~4,500 would need a complex several times the excavated one, for which there is no evidence.
- **Endowment check:** 200+ villages at the epigraphic rate of ~10–15 monks per village supports 2,000–3,000+ residents from Nalanda's own lands — consistent with, and only with, the low-thousands range.

**Peak resident population of Nalanda (7th century): c. 2,500–4,500, centred on ~3,000–3,500.** Total souls on the site at a peak moment — residents plus visitors, servants, and lay staff — plausibly reached ~5,000, which may be the real-world kernel that panegyric rounded up to a myriad. The 10,000 is excluded not by scepticism but by three independent instruments that agree with each other: the second pilgrim, the copper plate, and the ground.
